19th September, 2005.

Dear Edward,

It is with a heavy heart that I send this letter to you, and the thoughts within. I can only imagine the dreadful trauma you have had, and the sadness you and the children are feeling at this time.

Cindy was the most practical down to earth person I have known. She came to help me at home when she was 13. It was a Saturday job and Jenny would have been 2 years old.
Pat found my advert in the sweet shop by the co-op in Bosham.

Cindy was marvelous, she cycled down to me every Saturday morning, and it was lovely to see her, and discuss the weeks events and what she was doing at school. She stayed with me for quite a number of years, and I remember well, telling her I was pregnant again, this time with Michael.

The children's parties were made great fun with Cindy's organisation. She used to come armed with ideas for games, one of the most favourite was the chocolate game. I would be surprised if your children haven't played that one!

Cindy loved needlework. She made me countless peg bags out of scrape material, and I still have the sausage dog draught excluder she gave for my birthday one year. It is much treasured.

She so wanted to do home economics at school, and had to move schools to enable her to do this. You really have to admire her tenacity. I know she went to Surrey University.
Afterwards I believe she went back packing, and I think that is where she met you
Edward. I remember her telling me about you.

I thought you and she had really got it all sorted when you stayed at home with the children and she did teaching, it seemed a very good arrangement, and worked well, you were so good with them.

I am sure the years you have spent bringing the children up, has been a huge advantage, and will help with your future relationships with them, it can only be of benefit. You and they can be of great comfort to each other. I know too that you all have great faith, and follow the Quaker religion, and that is wonderful.
